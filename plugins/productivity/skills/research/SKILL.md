# Research

An answer is only as strong as the evidence chain under it. Trace claims to
original evidence, count only independent sources as corroboration, and keep
what was measured apart from what anyone concludes from it.

## 1. Pick the shape

Match the request to one shape, or two when a decision rests on a contested
claim. The answer adds that shape's elements.

| Request | Shape | The answer adds |
|---|---|---|
| Is this true? | **Fact-check** | Claim split into testable parts, each *supported / partly supported / unsupported / contradicted / unverifiable*; accurate rewrite keeping the uncertainty |
| What does this number mean? | **Trace** | Original source; date, population, sample, definitions, calculation, baseline; absolute vs relative change; one accurate shareable sentence |
| What should I read? | **Reading list** | Up to 12 sources, primary first, tabled by date, finding, limitation; top 3 with reasons |
| Explain this paper | **Paper** | Methods, sample, key numbers, limitations; what it supports, cannot establish, and who it applies to |
| How do these compare? | **Compare** | Table of claims, methods, populations; each disagreement traced to definitions, timing, or design; what stays unresolved |
| What's the debate? | **Debate** | Each side's best evidence and counterevidence; facts apart from values; the finding that would most change the picture |
| Why does this happen? | **Cause** | 2–4 causes with mechanism and evidence for and against; correlation vs causation; strongest mechanism step by step |
| Help me decide | **Decide** | 2–4 options against the user's priorities; conditional recommendation, what would change it, next 3 actions |
| Tell me about X | **Survey** | The 3–5 questions that matter most, in order; what the field has not settled |

Deliver a research plan instead only when the user asks for one, or the request
is too ambiguous to answer even as a Survey: 5 ranked questions, the evidence
each needs, your assumptions, an ordered checklist.

## 2. Gather evidence

- **Go upstream.** Reach the original study, dataset, or official document for
  each load-bearing claim. News, blogs, and encyclopedias point to evidence;
  they are not it. If you cannot reach the original, say so beside the claim.
- **Cite only what you opened.** A source you recall but did not open this
  session is marked unverified. No search tool available? Say so in the bottom line.
- **Count shared evidence once.** Three outlets reporting one study are one source.
- **Search against yourself** at least once.
- **Check dates.** Flag evidence that newer work has superseded.
- **Scale to stakes.** A trivia check may take two searches; a decision takes as
  many as reaching primary evidence requires.

## 3. Write the answer

1. **Bottom line**: 1–3 sentences, with your confidence.
2. **Shape elements** from the table.
3. **Findings**: source and evidence type (RCT, meta-analysis, observational,
   survey, opinion) for each, as stated by what you read, or *unknown* if you
   did not see the methods; what was measured, then how it is interpreted.
4. **Your judgment**, in its own section, when the shape calls for one.
5. **Limits**: what you could not verify; who the findings may not cover.
6. **Sources**: primary first, secondary labelled.

Length follows the stakes. A quick question gets the bottom line, its shape
elements, sources, and only the limits that change the answer.

Before sending, check every load-bearing claim names a source you opened or is
marked unverified, and every shape element is present.
